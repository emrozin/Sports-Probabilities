How this works

Every remaining game gets a random result from the model: its expected margin plus the usual game-to-game spread. Each simulated season also shifts every team's rating by a random amount (typically about {{ rating_sd }} points), because ratings are estimates, and a team's real strength can be quite different from what a dozen games show.

The top two teams in conference play meet in the title game (in the Sun Belt, the East and West division winners). Ties are settled by head-to-head record among the tied teams, then overall record, then the model's rating. That's close to, but simpler than, each conference's real tiebreakers. The title game is played at a neutral site for the ACC, Big Ten, Big 12, SEC, Pac-12 and MAC, and at the higher seed's stadium for the rest.

Tested on the 2024 and 2025 seasons (72 conference races, odds made before weeks 1, 5, 9 and 12): the eventual champion was given about a 13% chance on average, against 7% for picking a team at random, and 23% by week 12. The chances are honest: teams given 15–30% won 20% of the time, and 30–50% won 34%. A dash means it didn't happen in any simulation; that's not the same as being eliminated.
