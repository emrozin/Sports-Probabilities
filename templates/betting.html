What we tried to make it better

Each change was tried on the earlier seasons first, then checked once on the held-out seasons it never saw. Only betting at the best price helped, and only in the Premier League (the one sport with more than one bookmaker's price); it's what the rule uses now. Nothing turned a loss into a profit.

Blend mixes the model's chance with the market's before looking for an edge, weighted by what predicted the earlier seasons best. It backfired: with the chances pulled toward the market, the only edges left were long shots, where the bookmaker's margin is biggest, and those lost. Tuned rules looked good on the seasons they were tuned on (the grey rows) and fell apart on new ones, which is what overfitting looks like. The blend's held-out log loss is no better than the market's alone: the models don't know anything the prices don't already include. Better bets need better information, not a cleverer rule.

How the stakes are worked out

Expected return, Kelly and why only a fraction

Expected return. A bet at decimal price d (American +150 is 2.50) on a result with chance p returns p × d − 1 per $1 on average. The rule suggests a bet when that's {{ min_edge }} or more by the model's chances, at most one bet per game.

Kelly. If the chances were exactly right, staking (p × d − 1) / (d − 1) of your bankroll grows it fastest over the long run. But the chances are estimates, and full Kelly on overestimated edges loses money fast (see the past seasons above), so the rule uses a fraction of it ({{ (fraction * 100)|round|int }}% here) and never more than {{ cap }} of the bankroll on one bet.

Flat stakes. The live record counts $1 on every bet ("units"), the simplest fair test.
